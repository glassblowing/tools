## What it is

Murano's glass museum lists *pinse* (Italian *pinze*), tweezers, as the tool "used to pull, squeeze and shape hot glass".{% include cite.html n=1 %}

## How it's used

Wikipedia sums them up as the tool used "to pick out details or to pull on the glass".{% include cite.html n=3 %} They grab and pinch the glass, usually to stretch it, and for sculpting.{% include cite.html n=2 %} Points vary: BC Metalworking makes beaked points for grabbing and pulling, or a smoother flat point on request.{% include cite.html n=4 %}

## History

On Murano the pinching tweezers were traditionally *borsee da pisegar*, from *pisegar* (Italian *pizzicare*), "to pinch".{% include cite.html n=2 %} The Conciatore lexicon notes that the old *borsee da pisegar* curved slightly inward to a sharp point, as distinct from flatter *pinse*, though in the US both are simply called tweezers.{% include cite.html n=2 %}

## Care

Tweezers made of high-carbon spring steel will rust if not looked after; BC Metalworking advises drying them after use and wiping on a thin coat of a natural oil such as linseed or mineral oil.{% include cite.html n=4 %}
