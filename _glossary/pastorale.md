## What it is

The *pastorale* is a tool with a very long handle, forked at one end to hold a plate, with a T at the other end so it can't roll and drop everything.{% include cite.html n=1 %} Spiral Arts' version, for example, is 60" long, weighs 13 pounds, and holds a 16"-long plate.{% include cite.html n=2 %}

## How it's used

The long handle lets the plate go into the furnace or glory hole to preheat the canes laid on it.{% include cite.html n=1 %} Glass artist David Patchen, who heats plates of cane and murrine this way, describes the trade-offs in building one: solid forks are too heavy unless made from thinner stock, which then tends to be too weak; hollow forks can bend; and mild steel spalls and weakens under intense heat.{% include cite.html n=3 %}

## History

The name comes from *pastore*, "shepherd": it's the shepherd's staff, or crosier.{% include cite.html n=1 %}
