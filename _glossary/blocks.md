## What it is

Blocks are ladle-like tools made from water-soaked fruitwood.{% include cite.html n=1 %} The carved wooden head is fitted with a handle;{% include cite.html n=2 %} Jakobsson's come in a classic oval, a rounder shape for gathering, and an oversize oval.{% include cite.html n=3 %}

## How it's used

They're used much like the marver, to shape and cool a piece in the first steps of making it.{% include cite.html n=1 %} They only work wet: when hot glass touches the wet wood, a cushion of steam forms between them.{% include cite.html n=2 %} Blockhead Tools advises keeping wooden blocks submerged in water as much as possible (a quarter cup of household bleach per 5 gallons keeps mold away), dipping them often while working, and burning a new block in with a couple of clear gathers to build up a smooth charred layer.{% include cite.html n=2 %}

Because a block is held in one hand, weight and balance matter; Jakobsson chooses dense alder partly because it holds water well without being heavy.{% include cite.html n=3 %}

## History

Blockhead Tools began in 1989, when Rob Jones started making blocks for glassblowers in the Pacific Northwest in a barn on the Olympic Peninsula.{% include cite.html n=2 %} Today it carves its block heads from native bigleaf maple, chosen because it carves smoothly and soaks up the right amount of water for the steam cushion.{% include cite.html n=2 %}
