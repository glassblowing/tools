## What it is

The plate is a flat rectangle that canes are laid on to be preheated before they're picked up on a collar on a blowpipe; in the US almost everyone calls it a plate.{% include cite.html n=1 %} *Ferretti* are two short square bars, about 15 cm long and 1 cm square, set on either side of the canes so they don't roll off.{% include cite.html n=1 %} Correll sells ferros, 6" × 12" and 8" × 18" plates 3/8" thick, and ferriti in sets of four.{% include cite.html n=2 %}

## How it's used

Because hot metal sticks to glass, the surface of the plate where the canes lie is coated with wet clay and dried first, so the canes stay separate from the metal.{% include cite.html n=1 %} The plate goes into the glory hole on a [pastorale](/glossary/pastorale/).{% include cite.html n=1 %}

## History

On Murano the plate is the *piera*, from *pietra*, "stone": before large iron plates, glassmakers used a flat refractory brick, and before that, stones high in silica from quarries near Verona.{% include cite.html n=1 %} The *ferretti* (from *ferro*, iron) were once narrow pieces of refractory too.{% include cite.html n=1 %}
