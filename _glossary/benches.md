## What it is

The bench is the glassblower's workstation, with a place to sit, room for the hand tools, and two rails that the pipe or punty rides on while the blower works.{% include cite.html n=1 %} Murano's is the *scagno*, a backless wooden bench with a very wide seat and two long armrests, the *bardelle*, on which the master rolls the pipe; it's wide because it has to hold all the hand tools.{% include cite.html n=2 %} Spiral Arts' studio bench, for example, has 30.5" high rails 34" apart and a separate side table with hooks for tools.{% include cite.html n=4 %}

## How it's used

Seated, the glassblower rolls the pipe or punty back and forth along the rails while working the glass.{% include cite.html n="1,2" %}

## History

The bench is younger than you might think. Corning's study of Renaissance Venetian glassworking notes that a 1570 painting of the Medici glass workshop in Florence predates the glassblower's bench by at least a hundred years: the glassblowers in it have a narrow board strapped to the right thigh, and roll the pipe or punty back and forth against that.{% include cite.html n=3 %}
