## What it is

Parchoffi are [jacks](/glossary/jacks/) that have two wooden rods in place of metal blades, fitted into two short tubes at the end of the spring.{% include cite.html n=1 %} The rods are replaceable; Cutting Edge, for example, sells cherrywood rods separately.{% include cite.html n=2 %}

## How it's used

They're used wet, otherwise the wood burns too quickly.{% include cite.html n=1 %} They're for working hot glass without leaving marks, for instance opening a large thick vase or a low bowl from the inside, where the pointed blades of metal jacks would cut a deep groove.{% include cite.html n=1 %}

## History

On Murano they're called *borsee a parciofi* or *a paciofi*, usually just *paciofi*; American glassblowers mostly use the Muranese word, sometimes shortened to *'ciofi*.{% include cite.html n=1 %} The origin of the word isn't clear; one suggestion recorded in the Conciatore lexicon is *pacion* or *pacioso*, "chubby".{% include cite.html n=1 %} Makers spell it several ways, such as Cutting Edge's "Parchoffi (aka Pacioffi)".{% include cite.html n=2 %}

The wood varies by country: wild cherry in the United States, wild pear in Italy, Germany and elsewhere in Europe, for parchoffi and most other wooden hot-shop tools. These fruitwoods don't burn quickly against very hot glass and form a thin layer of carbon on the surface.{% include cite.html n=1 %}
