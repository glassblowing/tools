## What it is

Parchoffi are [jacks](/glossary/jacks/) whose metal blades are replaced by two wooden rods, fitted into short tubes at the ends of the spring. The rods are replaceable, and some makers offer graphite versions.

## How it's used

They're used wet, so the wood chars only slowly, and they're for working hot glass without leaving marks: opening a large thick vase, or pushing a low bowl open from the inside. Pointed metal blades would cut a narrow groove into soft glass doing the same thing; rounded wood spreads the glass instead.

## History

On Murano they're *borsee a parciofi* or *a paciofi*, usually shortened to *paciofi*; American glassblowers mostly use the Muranese word, sometimes cut down to *'ciofi*. The origin of the word is uncertain; one suggestion in the Conciatore lexicon is *pacioso*, "chubby".

The wood is chosen for how it burns. American shops use wild cherry, and Italian, German and other European shops use wild pear, for parchoffi and most other wooden hot-shop tools, because these fruitwoods char slowly and build a thin protective layer of carbon.

## What to look for

- **Rod size and wood:** cherry is standard in the US; replacement rods are sold separately.
- **Handle style:** round handles or square-back frames, and screw-on handles to fit your own.
