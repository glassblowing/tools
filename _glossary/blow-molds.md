## What it is

A blow mold is a hollow form that a bubble is blown into to give the piece its form; molds can be wood, clay, or metal{% include cite.html n=4 %}, and graphite is used too.{% include cite.html n=2 %}

## How it's used

Molds allow quick, uniform reproduction of a shape, size, or design.{% include cite.html n=4 %} With wood, the mold is used wet: when the hot glass meets the wet wood a thin layer of steam forms, which "polishes" the glass as it turns in the mold, and drilled vents let excess steam escape so the glass takes the exact shape.{% include cite.html n=1 %} Wood also doesn't chill the glass the way graphite or ceramic does, which matters for large pieces.{% include cite.html n=1 %} It does burn and wear: Jakobsson estimates anything from a hundred to a few hundred pieces, depending on the design and the blower.{% include cite.html n=1 %}

Graphite is far more durable and holds fine detail; bare graphite leaves a "chill mark" texture, and smooth pieces need the mold corked.{% include cite.html n=2 %} Blockhead's care advice for wooden molds is the same as for blocks: keep them submerged in water and burn them in with a couple of clear gathers.{% include cite.html n=2 %}

## History

Blowing a bubble inside a mold seems to have been an early first-century A.D. development, practiced in both the eastern and western Mediterranean.{% include cite.html n=3 %}
