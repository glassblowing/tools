## What it is

An optic mold is a small dip mold with a patterned inside, such as ribs or diamonds.{% include cite.html n=2 %} Murano's *rigadin* molds are open dies, generally of bronze, with grooves of triangular cross-section.{% include cite.html n=1 %} Molds are described by height and number of points (ribs): Hegan's run from 10 to 24 points, with ribs that meet in a closed, concave bottom or an open, flat one,{% include cite.html n=3 %} and Carlo Donà's Murano optical molds come in 12 to 30 points.{% include cite.html n=4 %}

## How it's used

The glassblower lowers the gather into the mold, inflates it against the walls, and lifts it out; then keeps blowing and shaping, and the pattern softens as the glass expands.{% include cite.html n=2 %} Twisting the ribbed gather makes a swirl,{% include cite.html n="1,2" %} and on Murano twisting it again the other way after reheating, then going back into the mold, gives criss-cross ribbing.{% include cite.html n=1 %}

Other pattern molds work the same way: dip molds can carry diamonds instead of ribs,{% include cite.html n=2 %} and Jim Moore's spring mold is a 5/16" steel coil whose pattern you have to "screw" the glass out of.{% include cite.html n=5 %}
